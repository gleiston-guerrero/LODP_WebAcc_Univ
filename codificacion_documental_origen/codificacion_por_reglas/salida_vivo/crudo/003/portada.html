Harvard and the Military

Since colonial times, Harvard has had strong ties to America’s military, and continues to advance scholarship in defense, leadership, and diplomacy.

Learn more about Harvard’s military legacy

Generations of service

Dating back to the American Revolution, Harvard University has shared a deeply interwoven history with the U.S. military.

Learn more about these ties

  • 1776

    During the American Revolution, students were dismissed early and the Harvard campus was turned over to the Continental Army. More than 1,600 soldiers were housed in Harvard buildings.

  • 1861

    Harvard undergraduates joined the 20th Massachusetts Volunteer Regiment, nicknamed “the Harvard regiment.” On campus, Harvard students rushed to protect the cannons and ammunition held at the Cambridge Arsenal.

  • 1941

    During World War II, the U.S. Naval Reserve operated a Supply Corps School at Harvard for the training of officers, including members of the Women Accepted for Volunteer Emergency Service (WAVES).

On campus and beyond

From academic resources to dental care, a variety of Harvard programs focus on veterans and their needs.

Providing care

To help address a critical gap in care for veterans, students at Harvard School of Dental Medicine host the annual “Give Veterans a Smile” clinic, which provides free dental care to veterans and their spouses.

Providing care

Preparing for service

Harvard was home to the first Army Reserve Officers’ Training Corps (ROTC) battalion, formed in 1916. In recent years, there has been an increase in students interested in military service.

Preparing for service

Supporting scholarship

Harvard Kennedy School’s American Service Fellowship provides full scholarships for public servants and military veterans for a one-year master’s degree.

Students in Harvard Law School’s Veterans Law and Disability Benefits Clinic work to protect the rights of veterans and their families.

Research and innovation

Members of the Harvard community are working to solve the problems and alleviate the issues encountered by veterans and the U.S. military.

Explore Maithili's work

Life-saving treatment for wounded soldiers

Harvard bioengineering graduate Maithili Joshi developed an injectable, self-assembling peptide that rapidly targets internal bleeding, binds to platelets, and speeds clotting without disrupting normal blood function, offering a new treatment for one of the leading causes of preventable trauma death.

Learn more about the research

Safer surgery in the field

Incubated at MIT and Harvard, SurgiBox SurgiField is a portable operating room that creates a safe environment to treat patients in war zones and disaster-affected areas.

Military radio frequency monitoring

Three Harvard School of Engineering students created Distributed Spectrum, a low-cost network of radio-frequency sensors that detects and locates signal interference, helping the military identify jamming or electronic attacks and protect communications.

ALS risk research

Researchers at Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health are studying veterans’ risk of ALS, a fatal nervous system disease.

Mobility challenges

Inspired by injured soldiers, former U.S. Army airborne engineer Bradley Scott Wagman co-founded Sole 1, which uses embedded robotics to help people with mobility challenges walk with confidence.

Veterans at risk of suicide

Harvard Medical School’s Ronald Kessler is developing prediction models to try to pinpoint who is at risk of suicide and determine the best ways to help them. The work has enormous potential to save veterans’ lives.
