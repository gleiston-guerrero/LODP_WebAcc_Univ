Two Delft consortia receive NWA-ORC funding for research with societal impact

Eleven large-scale research consortia will each receive approximately €7.4 million through the Research along Routes by Consortia (ORC) programme of the Dutch Research Agenda (NWA).

TU Delft and police terminate covenant

TU Delft and the police have jointly decided to terminate the covenant that governed cooperation between the two organisations. This covenant formed the basis for the exchange of information, including the personal data shared with the police in 2024 prior to a demonstration.

New living lab NatuurBuurt puts nature-inclusive building into practice

On Tuesday 27 October, the new living lab NatuurBuurt was opened on the grounds of The Green Village on the Delft University of Technology campus. Here, researchers, businesses and public authorities can test and further develop innovations that better integrate nature and biodiversity into the built environment.

Delft researchers control the magnetic core of a single atom, against expectations

Researchers at Delft University of Technology have succeeded in controlling the magnetism in the nucleus of a single atom with radio waves. This came as a surprise: until now, physicists believed that this nuclear spin could not be manipulated directly. The discovery could open up new possibilities for storing and reading quantum information. The research is published in the journal Nature Communications.

Vidi grant for thirteen leading TU Delft researchers

NWO has awarded 109 researchers a Vidi grant. Thirteen promising TU Delft researchers from the domains Exact and Natural Sciences (ENS), Social Sciences and Humanities (SSH) and Applied and Technical Sciences (ATS) have been awarded.

Soft robot arm powered by algae

Researchers from Delft University of Technology (The Netherlands) have created a small soft robot arm covered with minuscule algae that can grasp and lift objects.

The science behind world’s fastest autonomous racing drone

Researchers at Delft University of Technology (The Netherlands) have developed an AI system that enables a racing drone to navigate demanding courses at extreme speed using only a single camera. It outperformed even former world champion human drone-pilots in a race.
